Klarna launches AI ‘concierge’
The buy now, pay later provider has waded into agentic commerce with a new tool and aims to expand its longer-term loan portfolio.
Klarna Group introduced a personal artificial intelligence assistant this week to complement its core consumer lending business, and is debuting it through waitlist invitations, the buy now, pay later company said Tuesday.
“We are bringing AI and agentic commerce to the core of Klarna,” CEO Sebastian Siemiatkowski said Tuesday on a quarterly earnings call with analysts. Klarna users have spent about $34 billion since 2020 using the web browser feature in the existing app, the company said in a press release .
Separately , Klarna said it’s aiming to expand its longer-term installment loans to more U.S. and European consumers. Those loans are Klarna’s fastest-growing and “highest-earning product per dollar of volume,” the company said in its earnings release .
Klarna’s AI tool acts as “a personal concierge” for tasks such as booking reservations, drafting email replies, shopping and sending upcoming birthday reminders, the company said in its press release.
The assistant will not complete purchases autonomously – a human is required to authorize payments, a Klarna spokesperson said. Transactions through the AI tool are through a Klarna payment method or a user’s debit card, the spokesperson said.
The agent interacts via text message and is also available via Meta Platforms’ WhatsApp and Apple’s iMessage text services. The company spokesperson declined to say how many people have been invited to use the assistant so far.
The Klarna tool enters a crowded field of personal AI agents, with consumer offerings that include shopping abilities from Meta’s Muse, Google’s Gemini , Instinct, OpenAI’s dots and Daydream , a New York-based online search and shopping fashion startup. ByteDance, the Beijing-based parent of social media platform TikTok outside the U.S., also plans to release a personal AI agent, Bloomberg News reported last week .
As Klarna bots begin shopping, the company is keen to grow beyond its traditional zero-interest pay later loans. That retail-based lending still accounts for about three-quarters of the company’s gross merchandise volume.
Much of the growth for the lengthier “fair financing” loans has been in the U.S., where the company has partnered with merchants such as Airbnb, Walmart and Southwest Airlines .
Longer-term financing is available at 21% of Klarna’s affiliated merchants, or 283,000, up from about 150,000 a year ago, the company said.
Volume for these loans has more than doubled over the past 12 months, to $12.5 billion, compared to $6 billion in the rest of the world, Klarna said in its earnings report. The company also called out Europe as a region for growth. In the U.S., Klarna’s longer duration loans have terms up to three years and interest rates to a maximum of 35.99%.
For these loans, Klarna targets a transaction margin of 3% to 4% of volume, or “more than three times” what the traditional pay later loans earn, the company said in its earnings report.
For the third quarter, Klarna reported a net loss of $65 million , compared to a $95 million loss in the same period last year. Revenue rose 12% to $1 billion. Active users rose 7% from a year ago, to 122 million in 26 countries .
Klarna also projected a profit for the last three months of 2026, citing “a good start” to the quarter.
The earnings call was expected to be the last for Klarna CFO Niclas Neglen, who is leaving the company at year-end to become the chief financial officer at Adyen, the Amsterdam-based payments processor.
Last week, Klarna announced that Chris Koegel, a vice president at Robinhood Markets, the trading and personal finance platform, will become its new CFO effective Jan. 1.
