Data silos threaten banks’ competitive edge in AI
Nearly three in four consumers using AI for banking tasks opt for ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini — almost double those using a bank’s chatbot, a McKinsey report found.
Artificial intelligence is disrupting banks’ business models, prompting a race to reap the benefits before the window of opportunity closes, according to a McKinsey & Co. report published last week .
Nearly three in four consumers using AI for banking turn to OpenAI’s ChatGPT, Anthropic’s Claude or Google’s Gemini for banking tasks, almost double the amount who use a bank’s chatbot, the report found. The trend highlights a quickly fading grace period, as consumers across demographics embrace AI faster than previous technology waves, according to McKinsey.
As banks pour investment dollars into AI efforts, “speed could determine how much value the bank keeps, and unlike in past technology cycles, a late start may not offer chances to catch up, depending on how the profits get reinvested,” the report said.
As banks seek to strike a balance between accelerating AI use and maintaining operations and security, they’re also contending with digital technology challenges.
Thousands of customers from JPMorgan Chase, among other firms, reported issues accessing their online accounts Monday .
While navigating traditional technology challenges, data readiness continues to be an issue for chief information officers on their AI journeys.
Nearly half o f banking executives ranked data readiness as their top AI obstacle, McKinsey found. To see better results with AI projects over time, banks need to build institutional intelligence – starting with data that agents can consume at scale, according to McKinsey.
Banks also need to build context, which McKinsey described as a map of customer accounts with permissions set in place, and implement an encoded judgment call policy agents can reuse.
“The customer wants a capable deputy that asks for permission, and the bank that gets the permission model right may win the mandate to move the customer’s money,” the report said.
Still, high-value AI deployments remain stuck in pilot purgatory, largely due to meaningful business data also being trapped inside siloed legacy systems, according to a report from information management compan y Iron Mountain. Instead of spending time scaling agents, skilled IT and data science personnel are burning hours wrangling data manually to support projects, according to 84% of IT leaders.
In a move to improve analyst access to data, Northern Trust worked with Microsoft Frontier Company’s forward-deployed engineering team to launch an AI-powered onboarding agent. The agent cut data preparation time b y 83%, from 2½ weeks to three days.
“Accelerating our strategic execution and the transformation of the company is AI,” Northern Trust CEO Michael O’Grady said during the bank’s third-quarter earnings call in October. “Our focus has been on converting widespread adoption into business value.”
