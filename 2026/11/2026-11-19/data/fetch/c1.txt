Payhawk raised $280 million in a Series C round led by Goldman Sachs Alternatives, bringing its total funding to $515 million and valuing the company at $2 billion.
Payhawk plans to expand beyond corporate spend management by building an AI-powered Finance Operating System that combines autonomous financial workflows, payments infrastructure, and enterprise controls.
The funding reflects growing investor interest in AI-powered financial management, following recent raises by Clara and Ramp, as spend management platforms compete to offer more comprehensive financial automation capabilities.
Corporate payments orchestration platform Payhawk crossed a major milestone this week, raising $280 million in a Series C round that boosts the company’s valuation to $2 billion. The round, which brings Payhawk’s total funding to $515 million, is subject to regulatory approvals.
The investment was led by Growth Equity at Goldman Sachs Alternatives and included all of the company’s existing investors, including QED, Greenoaks, Lightspeed, Hedosophia, and Bek Ventures. New investors Galdana Ventures, Cogito Capital, Inveready and Invus, and entrepreneurs Tomas Okmanas and Didac Lee also contributed.
Founded in 2018 in Sofia, Bulgaria, Payhawk offers a financial management platform that helps businesses manage corporate spending, payments, and financial operations. Its platform combines corporate cards, expense management, accounts payable, procurement, and business travel with automated workflows and AI-powered tools. The company operates across 33 countries and has 12 offices worldwide. Payhawk has increasingly focused on using AI agents to automate financial tasks, including expense reconciliation, procurement, and financial reporting.
“The role of the finance team is rapidly moving away from manual checks, lengthy reconciliations and labor-intensive work towards becoming more strategic,” said Payhawk Co-founder and CEO Hristo Borisov. “While most companies use AI to speed up existing processes, we built AI agents to fundamentally rethink how finance operates.”
Payhawk will use the new capital to move beyond spend management and pursue its ambition of building “the global Finance Operating System for the AI era.” The company plans to combine AI agents that automate financial tasks, payments infrastructure that enables those agents to move money securely, and enterprise controls that establish permissions and determine when human approval is required. By combining these capabilities, Payhawk aims to help businesses automate financial workflows while maintaining oversight of transactions and spending.
Payhawk’s funding comes amid growing investor interest in corporate spend management platforms. Last week, Latin American spend management fintech Clara raised $105 million to expand its market reach and AI capabilities. Meanwhile, U.S.-based Ramp raised $1.85 billion last month at a $60 billion valuation as it expands its financial automation offerings. Both fintechs have outlined plans to move beyond corporate cards and expense tracking to offer more comprehensive financial management platforms.
Payhawk’s business model differs from some competitors because it emphasizes subscription revenue rather than card interchange fees. This approach allows the company to generate revenue from its software and automation capabilities rather than relying primarily on transaction volume. It also allows Payhawk to serve businesses that want to retain their existing corporate cards while adopting new financial management tools.
This distinguishing factor could be significant as AI automates expense management, payments, and other financial workflows because it will allow Payhawk to differentiate itself through the breadth and effectiveness of its financial automation capabilities.
