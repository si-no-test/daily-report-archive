Feedzai is expanding its Digital Trust solution to help banks detect, monitor, and control third-party AI agents accessing customer accounts.
The updated solution combines device fingerprinting, behavioral analysis, network intelligence, and cryptographic device binding to distinguish authorized AI agents from potentially malicious activity.
The new capabilities also strengthen customer authentication, helping banks reduce reliance on SMS one-time passcodes, prevent MFA bypass attacks, and minimize unnecessary authentication challenges.
Fraud prevention specialist Feedzai is expanding its Digital Trust solution to help banks detect and manage AI agents accessing customer accounts. The New York-based company is introducing new capabilities designed to distinguish authorized AI agents from potentially malicious automated activity.
The expansion comes as banks are challenged with customers using unauthorized third party AI agents to access and make changes in their accounts. Even though a customer’s agent may be accessing their account using valid credentials, that alone does not establish whether the activity is legitimate. Criminals can also exploit compromised devices, account recovery processes, and social engineering to gain access to customer accounts.
Feedzai’s Digital Trust addresses this issue by combining device fingerprinting, behavioral analysis, and network intelligence to assess risk before login and during a customer’s banking session. The technology recognizes returning devices and uses a fraud risk score informed by shared intelligence across Feedzai’s network to identify suspicious activity that banks might otherwise miss.
Along with today’s announcement, the company has also introduced cryptographic device binding, which links a customer, their device, and the bank. This helps banks establish which customer an agent is acting on behalf of and determine whether its activity should be permitted according to the bank’s guidelines.
Once the tool recognizes an AI agent, banks can apply their own policies to determine which activities to allow, challenge, or block. Digital Trust also allows banks to recognize previously trusted customer-agent relationships, which can reduce the need for repeated authentication while maintaining control over customer consent.
“A leading agent detection strategy starts with a foundation banks can trust: protected signals, cryptographic device recognition and network intelligence,” said Feedzai Chief Product Officer Pedro Barata. “Personal and malicious agents can look very similar. Our latest advancements in Digital Trust help banks tell them apart, blocking unwanted agents and trusting the ones their customers choose to use.”
In addition to detecting AI agents, the latest Digital Trust update also strengthens authentication for human customers. By confirming that a registered device is present and checking for signs of compromise, the technology aims to reduce reliance on SMS one-time passcodes and provide additional protection against attacks that bypass multifactor authentication. These capabilities extend to login, account recovery, new device enrollment, and call center authentication.
“Overreliance on weak multifactor authentication (MFA) is prevalent: 95% of banks use MFA, with one-time passwords (OTPs) sent via SMS as the most commonly cited method by Gartner clients,” said Vatsal Sharma, Director Analyst on the Banking team at Gartner. “However, growing tactics like social engineering, SIM swapping, and phishing can bypass such MFA, creating a false sense of security and requiring layered controls.”
Feedzai will expand its agentic detection capabilities in the coming months.
Feedzai was founded in 2011 as a risk operations platform specializing in identity verification, fraud prevention, and financial crime detection. The company’s AI-powered solutions span KYC, AML, watchlist screening, and transaction fraud monitoring to help financial institutions stop fraud in real time without compromising the customer experience. Today, Feedzai protects over one billion consumers in more than 190 countries and safeguards over $19 trillion in transactions annually.
