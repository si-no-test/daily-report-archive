Security and Resiliency Initiative
AI agents are moving from insight to action. Hyundai Card and Kinexys by J.P. Morgan , the firm’s industry-leading blockchain business unit of J.P. Morgan Payments , have collaborated to demonstrate that AI agents can safely participate in financial transactions when identity, delegated authority, controls, governance and auditability are directly embedded into institutional environments and payment flows.
The future of commerce will increasingly involve AI agents acting on behalf of people, businesses and machines. A key question remains: can AI agents make a transaction safely, responsibly and with accountability? This phased proof-of-concept aims to establish the trust framework required for future agent-to-agent transactions as part of an agentic economy.
Wee Kee Toh, Global Head of Kinexys Insights at Kinexys by J.P. Morgan , said, “Agentic commerce will not scale on autonomy alone. It will require trusted autonomy. Every autonomous action needs to be tied to a verified identity, a clear principal, a signed mandate and enforceable controls. This proof-of-concept shows how Kinexys can help provide the foundational trust, governance and execution framework that institutions need to explore AI agent transactions – whether that be one-to-one or one-to-many – in controlled environments. Starting with treasury, we see potential for this framework to support more complex agent-to-agent transaction models across connected commerce and machine-to-machine ecosystems.”
Phase one focused on a treasury management use case in a test environment with no live value movement. Under a digitally signed AI agent mandate (“the mandate”), a Hyundai Card AI agent was tasked with autonomously managing Euro liquidity in Hyundai Card’s Kinexys Blockchain Deposit Account in Frankfurt and requesting foreign exchange (FX) executions to maintain liquidity levels when required. The objective was to prove that AI agents can act on behalf of institutions under a signed, machine-readable mandate, while remaining subject to verified identity, delegated authority, institutional controls, human escalation and full auditability.
In this use case – explored in further detail in this paper – Hyundai Card’s AI agent autonomously monitored the Blockchain Deposit Account balance, identified a Euro shortfall and determined that a Sterling Pound to Euro FX conversion was required to maintain the liquidity levels set out in the mandate. Hyundai Card’s AI agent then requested FX quotes and then engaged Kinexys for execution. Kinexys’ own AI agent then verified the identity and authority of the Hyundai Card AI agent and evaluated its request against the mandate and applicable controls. Based on this evaluation, the Kinexys AI agent could either auto-execute, escalate to a human for review, or deny the request. Following the evaluation, the Kinexys AI agent auto-executed the request and a simulated FX transaction was booked. Crucially, the complete reasoning and decision data was captured in an immutable audit trail, providing visibility into the end-to-end transaction flow.
A Hyundai Card spokesperson said, “This proof-of-concept with Kinexys by J.P. Morgan is significant in that it validated a next-generation financial transaction model in which AI agents carry out corporate treasury management and foreign exchange transactions without direct human intervention. Building on our accumulated experience in payments in the mobility sector, we plan to expand our collaboration with Kinexys by J.P. Morgan into new areas such as payments in vehicles.”
The proof-of-concept establishes key properties for the trust framework:
Identity : AI agents’ requests must be verified in every instance.
Delegated authority : Every action ties back to a principal, agent and digitally signed mandate that is validated.
Controls : Execution is subject to deterministic controls, mandate validation and human intervention when required.
Explainability : The decision process remains visible from goal to plan to action to validation to outcome.
Auditability : A complete, immutable record is maintained for review by institutional clients, auditors and regulators.
