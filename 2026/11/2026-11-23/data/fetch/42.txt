StraitsX , OCBC and Standard Chartered have completed a proof of concept (PoC) to settle USD payments between two banks’ separate tokenized deposit solutions, outside banking hours and in minutes. The initiative is part of Singapore’s Project BLOOM , which supports settlement between different types of digital money. The news comes a day after the Monetary Authority of Singapore (MAS) published its tokenized deposit guide. And the same day Standard Chartered formally launched its tokenized deposit offering in Singapore, its second jurisdiction after Hong Kong.
Banks such as OCBC and Standard Chartered have single bank tokenized deposit platforms, meaning that payments between the two platforms, which may use different technologies, require coordination and interbank settlement. For Singapore dollars, it makes sense to use the RTGS system or wholesale CBDC settlement via the SGD Testnet, which MAS is renaming SG Digital Ledger. But that doesn’t work for US dollars, which is the example here. So StraitsX provided a shared platform to coordinate the payments end to end, from compliance screening through to settlement and confirmation. The PoC participants claim it reduces the need to provide bilateral prefunding.
What’s interesting is that StraitsX describes itself as a stablecoin-native settlement layer, and is known as a stablecoin issuer. Yet stablecoins were not mentioned in the PoC description at all. Something has to sit between the two banks and square them up. Stablecoins could potentially serve as that settlement instrument. But the mechanics imply a hub that holds balances at both banks, swapping tokens or triggering mints and burns on each platform. The new MAS tokenized deposit guide describes this kind of arrangement but is not prescriptive on the interbank settlement asset.
